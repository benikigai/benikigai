# Fictiv and Xometry: competitive brief

N+1 Labs research. Nov 6, 2026. Sources at the bottom.

## Bottom line

Both companies sell parts. A CAD file goes in, a part comes out, made in someone else's factory. Neither starts from an idea, and neither checks whether the product works. Both are moving upstream toward the language N+1 Labs uses, with far more capital behind them.

## Xometry

Founded 2013, North Bethesda, Maryland. Public since June 30, 2021 (Nasdaq XMTR).

### Founders and leadership

- **Randy Altschuler, co-founder.** CEO from 2013 to June 2026, Executive Chair since July 1, 2026. Born in New York City in 1970 and raised by a single mother. Worked his way through Princeton as a short-order cook and campus security guard (BA German Studies, 1993). Fulbright in Vienna, Harvard MBA (1998). Investment banking at DLJ, private equity at Blackstone. Co-founded OfficeTiger in 1999, an India-based back-office outsourcing firm sold to RR Donnelley in 2006 for $250M. Co-founded CloudBlue, an e-waste recycler sold to Ingram Micro in 2013. Ran for Congress in New York's 1st district as a Republican in 2010 and lost by 593 votes, the last House race decided that year. Ran again in 2012 and lost. Largest individual long-term Xometry shareholder. Sits on the board of OpenLane.
- **Laurence Zuriff, co-founder.** Early CFO, later Chief Strategy Officer, now on the board. Brown BA, Johns Hopkins SAIS MA. Analyst at SAIC and a summer fellow in the Pentagon's Office of Net Assessment. Then managing director and tech analyst at Weiss, Peck & Greer, and managing partner at Granite Capital. Two decades managing money in public equities.
- **Sanjeev Singh Sahni, CEO since July 1, 2026.** Previously Xometry's President. Succession announced Feb 24, 2026.

### Origin

While CloudBlue was being sold, Altschuler and Zuriff got interested in 3D printing. They found custom manufacturing fragmented, local, and opaque on price. The idea was a marketplace that uses data science to price jobs and route them to small shops the internet had never reached.

### Founder pattern

Operators and financiers, not engineers. Two prior exits in outsourcing and recycling. They think in marketplace liquidity and pricing, not product development.

### Scale

- Q3 2026 revenue $252M, up 40% year over year. Marketplace $238M, up 43%.
- First nine months of 2026: $687M. FY2021 $218M, FY2024 $546M.
- First GAAP profit in Q3 2026, $1.9M.
- About 90,000 active buyers (June 2026), 5,000+ active suppliers.
- $248M follow-on raise in 2026. Siemens bought about $50M of stock. $517M cash and securities at June 30, 2026.

### Model

Broker marketplace. An AI Instant Quoting Engine sets the buyer price, a matching algorithm sets the supplier cost, and Xometry keeps the spread (about 36% marketplace gross margin). Also sells Thomasnet advertising and Workcenter, a cloud MES for suppliers. Growth is driven by enterprise buyers.

### 2026 moves

- Siemens partnership (May 2026). Xometry pricing and manufacturability checks built natively into Siemens Designcenter CAD and Teamcenter PLM, so engineers see price and feasibility while they design.
- AI process recommender across 20 manufacturing processes.
- New lead-time and cost-prediction models for enterprise buyers.
- Injection molding expansion and self-serve reordering.

### In their words

- "The AI-driven execution layer that translates design intent into intelligent sourcing decisions and production outcomes" (10-Q, Q3 2026).
- "A continuous digital thread from design decision to delivered part" (Siemens partnership release).
- "Make the world's manufacturing capacity accessible to all" (Sahni).

## Fictiv

Founded April 2013 in a San Francisco kitchen. Acquired by MISUMI Group for $350M cash, announced April 17, 2025.

### Founders

- **Dave Evans, co-founder and CEO.** Stanford BS in mechanical engineering. First hire at Ford's Silicon Valley Innovation Lab, part of Ford's Global Research and Advanced Engineering. Forbes 30 Under 30. Since January 2026 also President of MISUMI Americas. Wrote the early code and ran manufacturing himself.
- **Nate Evans, co-founder and CXO.** Dave's brother. Stanford, international relations and an MA in Chinese. Investment banking at Seven Hills Partners. Ran business, design, and fundraising in the early years. Leads customer experience and business strategy.

### Origin

At Ford, Dave sent the same prototype request to one shop from his Stanford, Ford, and Gmail addresses, and got three very different prices and lead times. Prototypes took 8 to 12 weeks. The MVP was one MakerBot. The test was whether it paid for itself in a month. That grew into a network of idle industrial machines ("Airbnb for manufacturing"), then a managed global supply chain.

### Founder pattern

The closest profile to Ben's. An engineer who felt the sourcing pain from inside a large OEM. But the experience is automotive R&D prototyping, not consumer-electronics ramp. Fictiv solved quoting and sourcing, not getting a product through a ramp.

### Funding

About $192M total through a $100M Series E in May 2022, led by Activate Capital. Investors include Accel, Bill Gates, Sinovation Ventures (Kai-Fu Lee), Intel, G2 Venture Partners, and Standard Investments.

### Scale at acquisition

About 400 employees, about 250 manufacturing partners in the US, Mexico, India, and China, 35M+ parts produced.

### Model

Curated, managed network. Higher prices than open marketplaces, with better consistency and a better interface. Strongest for multi-part assemblies and teams that change designs often.

### Moves since the acquisition

- Added welding, mechanical assembly, and electromechanical assembly (Dec 2025).
- Working toward full-BOM sourcing: custom parts plus MISUMI's catalog of standard components in one order.
- MISUMI Group context: a $1B global investment plan, 323,000 customers, and about 10,000 CAD files a day flowing into its digital services.

### In their words

- "One system that supports everything from idea to production" (Evans, MISUMI Americas launch letter).
- "I've spent my career building things... how hard we make it to actually build hardware" (same letter).
- "Hardware at the speed of software."

## What this means for the launch

- **S4 voiceover.** "I've spent 17 years in that gap" sits close to Evans's letter. Only the specifics separate it: Apple, iPad Pro, Shenzhen.
- **Poster line.** "From an idea in your head to a product in your hand" sits near MISUMI's "idea to production." "In your head" is the gap. The film has to show the idea existing before any CAD.
- **S7 voiceover.** "Every part sourced, every step specified" describes Xometry's product. Showing Plus One on the partner line is what makes it N+1's.
- **Phrases to avoid on screen.** Digital thread, design intent, prototype to production, AI-powered platform.
- **Positioning drift.** The GitHub profile says N+1 Labs "predicts where a hardware team's next factory build will fail, before tooling, fixtures and the line are committed." That is further from both companies and names a paying customer. The launch post says "everyone." Decide which one leads.

## Open questions

1. **Partners or competitors?** The claim boundary says N+1 executes with partner factories, which is what both companies are. A build package that runs on their networks makes them a channel.
2. **The squeeze.** Siemens is pulling Xometry earlier into design. Text-to-CAD tools are reaching toward manufacturing. The idea-to-validated-product gap is real and narrowing from both ends.
3. **Who pays.** Xometry's growth is enterprise. The film can sell the kid. The YC application needs a first paying customer.
4. **OPD being open.** If either company adopts it, that is distribution, and the format stops being only N+1's.
5. **Founder-market fit.** Xometry's founders never built hardware. Evans built prototypes, not ramps. Ben's line is products ramped for millions. That is the YC answer to "why you."
